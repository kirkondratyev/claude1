## D. Dalio All-Weather scorecard
Asset biases: equities = rising growth/falling inflation; nominal bonds = falling growth/falling inflation; IL bonds = falling growth/rising inflation; commodities/gold = rising inflation (commodities also rising growth); corporate credit = rising growth; EM USD debt = rising global growth/rising inflation.

**Full-year 2026 YTD pattern (to 2 Oct):** commodities +32% (BCOM), oil +70%, equities +12.8% (S&P) with EM +23.5% / Nikkei +31%, copper +18–29%, cash ~+3%, HY ≈ flat, TIPS −2%, Treasuries −2.6% (long −8%), IG −4.4%, gold ≈ flat (after −25% from Jan peak), Bitcoin negative. Winners are the **rising-growth + rising-inflation** assets (commodities, EM, cyclically-levered equities, copper); losers are the **falling-growth/falling-inflation** assets (nominal duration) and, notably, even IL bonds lost (real yields up). That is the overheating/late-cycle quadrant: nominal GDP 8.5%, EPS +30%, PCE 3.4%, Fed hiking. Gold's failure to rally with inflation (it fell as real rates rose) marks this as a **real-rate-driven tightening** (1981/2022 type), not a 1973 monetary-debasement type.

**Post-16-Sep-hike pattern (Sept/early Oct):** 10y +54bp to 5.29%, 30y 5.6% (24-yr high), long Treasuries and IG −2.7%, HY −2.5% with OAS +50bp, EM HC debt down, gold −6.6%, R2000 −5.3%, equal-weight lagging cap-weight by 4.5pp, REITs −10% from July high, 83% of S&P members >10% off highs, IPOs pulled, VIX still 15–16; S&P −0.5% held only by megacap AI; oil +14%. Growth-sensitive long-tail assets (small caps, credit, breadth, REITs, EM debt) rolled over while headline equities and commodities held: the tape shifted from "rising growth + rising inflation" toward **"rising inflation + decelerating growth" (stagflation/tightening-bite)** at the margin. The 2 Oct payroll miss (29k) and core PCE 3.0% then produced a textbook late-tightening reaction (yields −6bp, growth stocks up, hike odds down) — markets still trade bad news as good, i.e. they do not yet price a growth break.

**Mid-cycle adjustments:** 1995–96 (cuts 6.00→5.25%, no recession): S&P +21.4% next year; 10y note futures −6% in year after first cut (1995 and 1998). 2019 (2.50→1.75%): S&P +8.7% next 12m until COVID; gold +26% 24m after first cut (vs +31% 2000, +39% 2007). Lesson: insurance cuts are equity-positive and bond-negative; recession cuts are bond- and gold-positive.

## Synthesis (≈230 words)
The cross-asset tape is a late-cycle, real-rate-driven tightening with the first cracks of a growth break. Commodities (+32%), oil (+70%), EM/Japan and megacap AI have led; nominal duration (long UST −8%, IG −4%) and even TIPS have lost; gold fell with real yields rather than hedging inflation. Since the 16 Sep hike the growth-sensitive tail — small caps (−5% in Sept), breadth (83% of S&P names >10% off highs), REITs (−10%), HY spreads (+50bp), EM dollar debt, IPOs — has rolled over while headline equities and crude held. That is the "rising inflation, decelerating growth" transition, and the 29k payroll print, 51k three-month average and core PCE at 3.0% say growth is slowing faster than the market's 4.7% mid-2027 funds-rate path assumes.

**Most discounted:** more Fed hikes and no cuts through 2027; 15% 2027 EPS growth; "no landing" (93% of FMS); crowded long-semis/short-bonds. **Least discounted:** a recession (HY 312bp vs 800bp+ in recessions; ERP ≈ 0; VIX 15; no cuts priced), a bond rally (bonds deepest underweight since 2022), and an oil collapse (record CTA longs, steep backwardation).

**Most mispriced vs the tightening→early-recession archetype:** (1) long-end Treasuries — priced for 4.7–4.8% policy rates into 2028 with term premium rebuilding, while labour data decelerates; (2) HY credit/ERP — equities at 19x on +15% EPS with zero premium over the 10y leave no cushion for a −20% archetype move; (3) crude at $100+ with record speculative length, the asset that historically collapses first when growth breaks.
