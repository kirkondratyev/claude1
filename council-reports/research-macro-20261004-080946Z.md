## 1. Dubai residential market state (Q3/Q4 2026)
The cycle turned in 2026 and the trigger was geopolitical, not supply. The US–Israel/Iran war began 28 Feb 2026 with Iranian strikes across the Gulf (hotels and residential buildings in the UAE were hit); a ceasefire held 8 Apr–8 Jul, US strikes resumed 13 Jul, Iranian attacks on UAE tankers continued into Aug, and on 1 Oct a third US carrier group was ordered to the region (CNN 13 Jul, PBS, CNBC oil 1 Oct). Brent is $102 (2 Oct 2026, +59% y/y).

Prices: ValuStrat's VPI fell 5.9% m/m in Mar 2026 (first monthly fall since 2020), -1.9% Apr, -1.2% May (Consultancy-ME), then flattened; Aug 2026 reads -3.1% y/y, apartments -5.3%, villas -1.7% (first villa annual decline since 2021), 10.2% below February. Transaction-based averages show AED 1,636/sqft in Aug 2026, -1.7% y/y. Pre-war, Dec 2025 VPI was +19.8% y/y (villas +25.1%, apartments +14.2%) (ValuStrat Dec 2025). Listing-based indices still show positive y/y (Property Finder: +4.6% y/y Sep 2026) — treat these as lagging.

Volumes: Q2 2026 residential deals 34,850 (-31% y/y), AED 84.9bn (-45% y/y), avg price -5.9% q/q (Betterhomes Q2); Q3 2026 ~AED 93bn across ~37,000 deals (Harbor Q3) vs the Q3 2025 record of AED 170.7bn/59,228 (all sales). Sept 2026: AED 29.66bn/11,430 sales; ready (AED 16.03bn) outsold off-plan (AED 13.63bn) by value (truhauz).

Stress signals: Reidin average resale listing duration rose from 24 days (2023) to 61 days (Sep 2026) (GI Properties); sellers had cut asking prices by a combined AED 2.36bn by end-May with 12–15% discounts to recent asks (Reuters via Fortune, 1 Jun 2026). Population dipped 61,000 in March but hit a record ~4.73–4.74m by June (+157,000 YTD) (The National, 4 Aug 2026).

Downtown: Bayut index AED 3,327/sqft (Aug 2026), -0.35% y/y overall but 1-beds -4.22%, while 3-beds +6.2%. Verdict: apartments are in a mild-to-moderate correction (-3% to -5% y/y, -10% from peak) while villas/prime hold better; the market is "stabilising lower", not collapsing.

Fitch's call (29 May 2025): "moderate correction" of up to 10–15% in H2 2025–2026 driven by ~210,000 units due in 2025–26, double the prior three years (The National). What happened: prices kept rising through Feb 2026 (+19.8% in 2025), then fell ~10% from peak in Mar–Aug 2026 — roughly Fitch's magnitude but caused by the war, with the supply wave still ahead. Fitch (May 2026) now sees UAE GDP -4.8% and Dubai -7% in 2026 (AGBI); actual Q1 2026 UAE GDP was +3% (Gulf News). S&P expects apartments to fall more than villas and secondary supply to rise as investors exit (Gulf Business).

## 3. Dubai rental market 2026
CBRE: average rents -6.2% q/q and -2.6% y/y in Q2 2026; Downtown apartments -14% q/q (largest drops); Business Bay volumes -11% y/y (Gulf News). By summer, new-lease rents were 15.3% below January while renewals fell ~1%; in Aug new leases exceeded renewals by 342 in Downtown — tenants are switching rather than renewing (IndexBox). Conflicting data: Betterhomes Q2 rents +3.1% y/y, flat q/q, tenant enquiries +20%; Bayut H1 2026 Downtown 1-bed asking +3.35% to AED 99k. ValuStrat expects rental growth to stop by end-2026.

Smart Rental Index (RERA, Jan 2025): building star-rating plus tiered caps — no increase if rent is within 10% of index, up to 5/10/15/20% at 11–20/21–30/31–40/>40% below; tenants are using it to negotiate reductions (Khaleej Times). Gross yields (Aug 2026): Dubai 6.34%, apartments 6.66%; Downtown ~5.1%, Creek Harbour ~5.1%, JVC 6.4–9% (Aigents). Downtown service charges AED 18–30/sqft: for a 1,036 sqft 1-bed renting at AED 99–120k, net yield ≈ 3–3.8% on a ~AED 2.5m value. No 2026 vacancy/days-to-let series was found.

## 6. Tax
(a) UAE: no personal income, capital-gains or rental tax for individuals (PwC). Selling costs ~2–2.5%; the 4% DLD fee is usually borne by the buyer but negotiable in a buyer's market.

(b) Kazakhstan (new Tax Code No. 214-VIII, in force 1 Jan 2026): residency = permanent residence or centre of vital interests, incl. 183 days in any 12 months. IIT is now progressive: 10% up to 8,500 MRP (36.76m KZT ≈ $82k) and 15% above, computed on aggregate taxable income incl. foreign income (finance.kz). Foreign rental income is taxed at that scale, self-assessed in Form 270.00 by 15 Sep of the following year, tax due by 25 Sep (mybuh). Gain on foreign real estate = sale price minus documented acquisition cost; the 1→2-year holding exemption applies only to property located in Kazakhstan, so no holding-period exemption for the Dubai flat; if cost is not documented, the default is that the whole sale price is income (kodeksy-kz Art. 332, nur.kz). Important flag: Kazakhstan's preferential-tax-jurisdiction list (MoF Order No. 492 of 12 Sep 2025, effective 1 Jan 2026) still includes the UAE "in the part of the territory of Dubai" (cdb.kz) — verify with an adviser whether any list-related restriction applies. Foreign tax credit exists but UAE tax is zero. DTT: in force 27 Nov 2013, applying from 1 Jan 2014; immovable-property income and gains may be taxed in the situs state, with Kazakhstan taxing residents and crediting. Universal declaration: Form 270.00 remains mandatory for persons with assets abroad — a Dubai flat triggers filing.

## 8. Historical context
2008–10: Dubai prices fell ~50–60% peak-to-trough in ~18 months, flat through 2012; trigger global liquidity crunch/Dubai World debt. 2014–20: -29.3% from mid-2014 to H1 2019, with a further 2020 leg to 2012 lows (~6 years); triggers: oil collapse, strong dollar, CBUAE LTV caps and the 2→4% transfer fee (2013), supply of 25–35k units/yr vs ~20k absorption. 2020–26 boom: apartments +69–71% since Q1 2020, villas +124% (Knight Frank) to +206% (ValuStrat, 2025); drivers: golden visa (2021), early vaccination, global capital inflows, cheap money. Current cycle: trough ~Q4 2020 to peak Feb 2026 (~5.3 years, the longest upswing since 2002–08); correction is 7 months old and -10% so far, with the 2027–28 handover wave still to come. Past downturns lasted 2–6 years; consensus 2027 views are +1–5% (Knight Frank ~3% prime/1% mainstream) with a bear case of -10–15% for mid-market apartments.
